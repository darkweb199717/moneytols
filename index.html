Assumes fixed rates and monthly payments; the origination fee is deducted from proceeds, so the new loan is grossed up. Compare total costs even if the new payment is lower.

Frequently asked questions

Can consolidation save money?

It may reduce interest if the new APR and fees are low enough. Compare total cost and payoff time, not only the monthly payment.

What if I cannot afford a debt payment?

The calculator flags payments that do not cover monthly interest or require extremely long repayment. Contact creditors for options.

Does a debt consolidation loan always save interest?

No. A lower APR can still cost more if the new term is much longer or the origination fee is high. Compare total interest and fees as well as monthly payments.

Is a balance transfer the same as a consolidation loan?

No. Balance-transfer cards can use introductory rates with a deadline and fee; a consolidation loan generally has a fixed repayment schedule. This tool models the loan scenario.

Debt consolidation loan rates and debt payoff options

Comparing debt consolidation loan rates is about more than finding a smaller monthly payment. A longer loan term may lower what you pay each month while increasing total interest. Enter the balance, APR and payment for each credit card or existing loan to see how the combined debt compares with a new fixed-rate consolidation loan.

For a credit card debt consolidation calculator, use the actual card APR rather than a promotional rate that will expire soon. Include any loan origination fee, and check how long it would take to pay off the original balances at today’s payments. If a balance transfer card has a promotional 0% APR, compare its transfer fee and promotion expiration separately; this calculator models installment loan consolidation, not changing card promotional terms.

The debt payoff comparison shows estimated monthly payment differences and total cost differences. A positive monthly saving does not necessarily mean lower lifetime cost. If your original payments do not cover the monthly interest, increase the payment or discuss your options with the lender before relying on the results.

How to compare debt consolidation loan offers

Start with the payoff balances on your current debts, not the original amounts borrowed. Compare a personal loan for debt consolidation using the same payoff amount, the lender’s disclosed APR, origination fee and repayment term. If the lender deducts a fee from the disbursement, you may need to borrow more to clear every balance.

Illustrative example: A $15,000 fixed-rate loan at 12% annual interest over 48 months has a payment of about $395 per month before fees, assuming monthly amortization. A longer term generally lowers that payment but increases interest paid. This example is not a current rate quote.

Debt consolidation questions worth checking

Does consolidating credit card debt improve my credit? It depends on credit utilization, payment history, credit inquiries and whether you accumulate new balances. Is a lower payment always better? No. Compare total repayment cost and fees, not only the monthly amount.

Before comparing offers

  • Compare loan APR and origination fees: Use the prospective lender’s rate and upfront fees rather than an advertised “starting at” APR.
  • Check the full payoff period: Compare the remaining payoff duration on each existing debt against the proposed new term.
  • Separate eligibility from affordability: Credit score and lender approval can affect the rate offered; this tool does not check eligibility.

The refinance comparison calculates both amortization schedules, remaining balances, upfront costs and payments over the same selected horizon. Simple break-even is also shown, but can mislead when loan terms differ.

Frequently asked questions

What is the refinance break-even point?

It is an approximate number of months for monthly payment savings to recover upfront closing costs. It does not replace a full balance-adjusted comparison.

Does extending the loan term reduce total cost?

Not necessarily. A longer term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing lifetime interest. Compare remaining balances at a fixed horizon.

Is refinancing worth it if the payment falls?

Not always. Compare closing costs, the new repayment term and the outstanding balance at the same future date before deciding.

Does this model cash-out refinancing or PMI?

No. It models a rate-and-term refinance using the balance and financed closing costs entered. Cash-out proceeds, PMI, taxes and insurance need separate evaluation.

Mortgage refinance rates, closing costs and break-even

A mortgage refinance calculator can help evaluate a lender’s proposed refinance rate against your current mortgage. Enter the unpaid principal balance, current rate and remaining term, then compare the proposed interest rate and term. The results focus on principal and interest; property taxes, homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance are not included.

When evaluating mortgage refinance closing costs, distinguish amounts paid upfront from fees added to the new loan. Financing closing costs increases the new principal and changes total interest. A refinance break-even calculator based only on the monthly payment difference is a starting point, but it can be misleading when refinancing also extends the loan term.

For a more useful refinance savings estimate, compare the balance remaining on each mortgage and the payments over the same period. If you might sell or refinance again in a few years, choose a comparison horizon closer to how long you expect to keep the mortgage. This tool does not model cash-out refinance proceeds or adjustable-rate changes.

Calculate mortgage refinance break-even

A simple refinance break-even estimate divides upfront closing costs by monthly payment savings. For example, $3,600 in upfront costs and $150 in monthly savings suggests 24 months to recover those costs. This shortcut does not account for changes in principal balance, loan term, taxes or the time value of money, so review the full amortization comparison before deciding.

When comparing a 15-year versus 30-year mortgage refinance, check both the monthly budget and total interest. A shorter term often increases the monthly payment but can reduce lifetime interest. A lower refinance rate is not automatically a better deal if the loan term resets and you expect to move soon.

Mortgage refinance questions worth checking

Should I include points? Yes. Discount points and lender fees can change the true cost of the refinance. What if closing costs are financed? They increase the new balance and may reduce or eliminate expected savings.

Before comparing offers

  • Compare rate quotes on similar terms: A shorter or longer new loan term can change the payment independently of the interest rate.
  • Include both types of closing costs: Account for upfront fees and any amount financed into your new mortgage.
  • Evaluate when you might move: A lower monthly payment may not offset the fees before you sell the home.

The payment uses a fixed-rate amortization formula. Effective annual borrowing cost is based on net cash received after upfront fees and the monthly payments made.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator include loan fees?

Yes. Upfront fees reduce net proceeds and are reflected in the estimated effective annual borrowing cost.

Is this suitable for variable-rate or balloon loans?

No. The model assumes equal monthly payments on a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan.

What is the difference between interest rate and effective borrowing cost?

The interest rate is charged on the loan principal. An effective annual cost estimate also considers upfront origination fees and the timing of scheduled payments.

Can this calculate an SBA loan?

It can estimate a fixed-rate amortizing payment using supplied terms, but it does not calculate every SBA program fee or variable-rate provision.

Small business loan rates, financing fees and cash flow

Use this small business loan calculator to estimate what a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan may cost your business. Enter the amount borrowed, annual interest rate, repayment term and origination fee. The payment estimate can support a business cash-flow forecast, but it does not check your company’s borrowing eligibility.

For business loan interest rates, compare more than the quoted percentage. Upfront origination charges can reduce the cash your company receives even though payments are based on the full loan principal. The estimated effective annual borrowing cost accounts for this reduced net proceeds amount and regular monthly installments.

If you are researching SBA loan payment estimates, use the calculator only for a fixed-rate, fully amortizing scenario with the actual terms in your offer. SBA-backed lending may involve lender fees, variable rates, guarantees, additional charges or loan structures that are not fully represented here. For revolving business credit, balloon payments or variable-rate borrowing, request a lender-specific amortization schedule.

Estimate small business financing affordability

When using a business loan payment calculator, compare monthly debt service with expected free cash flow after payroll, rent, inventory and taxes. Stress-test a slower-sales scenario rather than relying only on an optimistic revenue forecast. Ask the lender whether payments are monthly, weekly or daily; this calculator assumes a conventional monthly amortization schedule.

Illustrative example: A $50,000 fixed-rate business loan at 10% annual interest over 60 months produces a monthly payment of about $1,062 before fees. A 3% origination fee would reduce net proceeds by $1,500 if deducted upfront, while the scheduled payment may still be calculated on $50,000. These are hypothetical terms, not advertised offers.

Business loan questions worth checking

Is an SBA loan the same as a conventional business loan? No. Program eligibility, guarantees, fees and repayment structures can differ. Does this tool calculate approval odds? No. Lenders separately assess revenue, credit, collateral and other underwriting factors.

Before comparing offers

  • Compare principal and fees: A fee reduces net cash available to fund inventory, payroll or equipment.
  • Budget for monthly payments: Compare the projected payment with cash flow during slower business months.
  • Look beyond the interest rate: Compare the effective annual cost and total interest alongside the stated loan rate.

Estimate how much home equity you may borrow and the monthly repayment for a fixed-rate home equity loan.

Enter your details

Available borrowing equals the selected maximum combined LTV multiplied by the home value minus the existing mortgage, floored at zero. Payments assume a fixed-rate amortizing home equity loan.

Frequently asked questions

What is combined loan-to-value?

Combined LTV is the existing mortgage plus the proposed home equity loan divided by the estimated home value.

Does available equity guarantee approval?

No. Credit, income, property value, lender limits, fees and other requirements affect eligibility.

What is a combined loan-to-value calculator?

It calculates (existing mortgage balance plus proposed home equity loan) divided by home value, expressed as a percentage.

Does this estimate a HELOC monthly payment?

No. HELOCs can have variable rates, draws and different payment periods. This tool is for a fixed-rate amortizing home equity loan.

Home equity loan rates, borrowing limits and combined LTV

A home equity loan calculator can help estimate how much of your property value may be available for a second loan. The calculation uses an estimated home value, existing mortgage balance and a combined loan-to-value (CLTV) limit that you choose. Available borrowing is an illustration, not a lending approval or an appraisal.

When comparing home equity loan rates, enter the APR or nominal annual rate that matches the lender’s proposed repayment terms, and check whether additional fees apply. The payment estimate assumes a fixed interest rate and equal monthly installments. Actual products can have closing costs, required insurance or other terms not included here.

A home equity loan versus HELOC comparison requires care: this calculator models a fixed-rate home equity installment loan, not a home equity line of credit with draws, variable APR or interest-only repayment. If you are considering debt consolidation with home equity, remember that the home secures the new borrowing and missed payments can put it at risk.

Estimate home equity borrowing with combined loan-to-value

Combined loan-to-value (CLTV) compares all loans secured by a home with the property's estimated value. For example, a home worth $400,000 with a $240,000 mortgage and a proposed $40,000 home equity loan has a CLTV of 70%. Lender limits, appraisal results, credit qualifications and fees determine whether the loan is actually available.

When comparing a fixed-rate home equity loan with a HELOC, consider payment predictability. A fixed installment loan generally has a scheduled payoff, while a HELOC may have variable rates, draw periods and changing minimum payments. Do not use a fixed-loan estimate as a HELOC quote.

Home equity questions worth checking

Can I borrow all my home equity? Usually not; lenders commonly apply borrowing limits and underwriting rules. What is the key risk? Your home secures the debt, so nonpayment can have serious consequences.

Before comparing offers

  • Understand available equity: Your total equity is not necessarily the amount a lender will allow you to borrow.
  • Check combined loan-to-value: CLTV includes the existing mortgage plus the proposed home equity loan.
  • Assess secured borrowing risk: A home equity loan is secured by your property; compare alternatives and total costs carefully.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a life insurance quote?

No. The premium is an illustrative scenario calculated from your own base rate and stated multipliers. It is not an offer or market price.

How much life insurance do I need?

A basic needs approach adds income replacement and obligations, then subtracts available savings and existing insurance. Your actual needs may differ.

Is this calculator a term life insurance quote?

No. It uses the annual per-$1,000 assumption entered by you with illustrative adjustments. Actual insurers price policies through their own underwriting.

How does tobacco use affect the estimate?

The scenario uses a simplified multiplier to illustrate a potential cost difference, not a rate table. Real price differences vary by insurer and applicant.

Term life insurance costs and coverage planning

This life insurance cost calculator helps explore a coverage amount before you request actual quotes. The coverage-needs estimate combines years of replacement income and other obligations, then subtracts savings and insurance already available. You can compare the resulting needs estimate with the coverage amount you are considering.

For term life insurance cost planning, enter a hypothetical annual cost per $1,000 of coverage, your age, term and tobacco assumption. The calculated premium is an illustrative life insurance premium estimate using stated assumptions and scenario multipliers; it is not based on current insurer pricing or underwriting. It must not be used to compare real insurer premiums.

To get life insurance quotes, contact licensed insurance providers or brokers and compare the same coverage, policy type and term. Health history, age, tobacco use, insurer, jurisdiction and riders can materially change actual premiums. This tool does not provide eligibility decisions, guaranteed issue or a binding quote.

Estimate how much term life insurance coverage to consider

A life insurance coverage calculator can start with replacement income, outstanding debts, childcare or education goals and final expenses, then subtract savings and existing coverage. For example, five years of $60,000 replacement income equals $300,000 before adding obligations or subtracting available assets. This is a planning illustration, not a recommendation for any particular household.

When researching term life insurance rates, compare the same coverage amount, term length and policy features across actual insurer quotes. A 10-year term and a 30-year term do not provide equivalent protection. Optional riders, underwriting class and renewal provisions can materially change premiums.

Life insurance questions worth checking

Is the estimated premium a real quote? No. This tool uses illustrative assumptions rather than live insurer pricing. What affects actual premiums? Factors may include age, health, tobacco use, term, coverage, underwriting and location.

Before comparing offers

  • Estimate coverage needs first: Consider income replacement, debts, final expenses, savings and existing coverage.
  • Compare like-for-like policies: Match term length and coverage amount when evaluating real quotes.
  • Treat cost scenarios as examples: Do not interpret the displayed illustrative monthly amount as an actual market premium.
